***REMOVED******REMOVED*** 1. Seismologist hat — Phase 1 (USGS earthquakes)

The Phase 1 gate test is the strongest part of the paper from a referee's standpoint: large modern catalog, standard Wiemer–Wyss completeness, Aki MLE with Shi–Bolt error, independent Clauset cross-check, 500-resample bootstrap, three-decade Omori fit with R² = 0.9927. A working seismologist will still surface the following.

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 1.1 P0 (must-fix-before-submission)

- **P0-S1. Catalog declustering — defend the choice, don't just assert it.** §3.1 says: "No declustering is applied at catalog construction time, since declustering would bias the Gutenberg-Richter fit being measured." This is *partially* defensible but not the consensus view. The standard seismological position is that the *background* b-value should be estimated on a declustered catalog (Reasenberg, Gardner–Knopoff, or ETAS-stochastic declustering), while *paired aftershock sequences* are analyzed on the un-declustered catalog for Omori. The C1 wording elides this. A real BSSA reviewer will ask: "What is your b-value on a Gardner–Knopoff-declustered subset, and how does it differ from the un-declustered 1.084?" If the difference is < 0.02 the claim is unaffected; if it is larger, the paper needs to report both. **Recommended fix: add a sentence stating either (a) declustering was tested and changed b by < 0.02 — preferred, requires re-running on the source data — or (b) the C1 claim is the un-declustered global b-value, which is the standard headline number, with the understanding that a declustered estimate would be a refinement.**
- **P0-S2. M_c = 4.45 is high — justify against the 2020-2025 catalog's actual cumulative-frequency curve.** For the global USGS catalog over 2020–2025 the literature M_c is typically reported in the 4.0–4.5 range, but modern Wiemer–Wyss on the full global catalog often returns 4.2–4.4 rather than 4.45. The 0.05–0.25 difference matters because n_above_Mc moves from ~37k (at 4.45) to ~80k (at 4.0), and the b-value bootstrap CI tightens correspondingly. The source paper's §2.2 says "for our 2020–2025 global catalog we obtain M_c = 4.45" and reports n = 37,281 — fine for reproducibility, but a reviewer will want to see the cumulative frequency-magnitude diagram (FMD) plot or the maximum-curvature output to confirm 4.45 is genuinely the maximum-curvature value and not a slight over-estimate. **Recommended fix: add the FMD plot to Appendix A, or at minimum state the cumulative-frequency value at M = 4.45 and at the next bin down M = 4.35 so the maximum-curvature step is auditable.**
- **P0-S3. Magnitude-type heterogeneity in USGS.** The USGS event service mixes Mw (where available), mb, ML, Md, and other magnitude scales without homogenisation. Mixing them without conversion to a single scale (usually Mw) systematically biases the b-value, typically *down* by 0.02–0.10 depending on the scale mixture. The Phase 1 source paper (§2.1) does not address this. A BSSA reviewer would consider this a structural issue. **Recommended fix: state the magnitude-type composition of the 37,281-event sample (Mw / mb / ML / Md percentages) and either (a) show that the result is stable on the Mw-only subset, or (b) acknowledge the homogenisation gap and cite Hutton et al. / Edwards & Fäh for the typical Mw-vs-mb conversion bias.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 1.2 P1 (should-address)

- **P1-S1. Spatial-window scaling for Omori is approximate.** §2.6 of the source paper uses r_km(M) = 10^(0.5M − 1.2), a coarse Utsu-form rupture-length scaling. A Wells–Coppersmith rupture-length-and-width mapping with focal-mechanism-dependent geometry would be the BSSA standard. The source paper concedes this. C1 v0.2 inherits the simplification silently. **Recommended fix: a one-sentence acknowledgment in §3.1 or §6.4 — already partially covered by §6.5 (single sessions / single windows) but not specifically the spatial-window approximation.**
- **P1-S2. The 0.07 difference between Aki-MLE-from-b and Clauset-on-energies (α_E = 1.794 vs τ = 1 + b/1.5 = 1.722) is reported as "consistent with the b/1.5 relation under Hanks–Kanamori scaling," but it is technically ~3σ on the Clauset analytic error. The source paper §3.1 calls this "the expected level of agreement given finite-sample noise and the different x_min choices." A referee may push: 3σ is "consistent" in this paper but is the threshold above which Phase 3's 0.12 DeFi α-spread is *not* called identical. **Recommended fix: unify the threshold language — either both 3σ-level disagreements are "consistent given different nuisance choices," or both are "technically distinguishable but not on a physically meaningful scale." Currently the paper uses the former for Phase 1 and the latter for Phase 3, which is internally inconsistent.**
- **P1-S3. Aki MLE on binned magnitudes — bin-correction.** The Aki estimator as written in the source paper (§2.3) uses ⟨M⟩ − (M_c − Δ/2) with Δ = 0.1, which is the binning-corrected form. The C1 v0.2 paper does not show this formula explicitly. **Recommended fix: include the binning-corrected Aki form in §2 or in Appendix A so a referee does not have to chase the source paper to verify the correction was applied.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 1.3 P2 (nice-to-have)

- **P2-S1. Regional disaggregation.** The source paper §5 calls this out as a limitation; C1 v0.2 §6.5 covers it generically. A per-Flinn-Engdahl-region b-value table would be a strong addition, but the absence is acceptable in a "pipeline gate test" framing.
- **P2-S2. Omori-c grid resolution.** §2.6 says "(p, c, K) by weighted log-log regression with c grid-searched." The grid resolution is not stated. **Recommended fix: footnote the grid (e.g. "c ∈ {0.01, 0.02, …, 1.0} d").**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 1.4 The hardest single question a seismology referee will ask

> *"You report b = 1.084 ± 0.005 on a magnitude-mixed un-declustered global catalog 2020–2025. The Mw-only declustered comparison would settle whether 1.084 is the physical b or a 0.02–0.05 inflation from the mixture. Have you run it? If not, why not?"*

This is the question to be ready for. The defensible answer is either (a) the comparison has been run and changes b by < 0.02, or (b) the C1 claim is the standard global headline number and the cleaner Mw-only declustered estimate is a Phase 1 follow-up beyond C1's scope. Both are acceptable; silence on the question is not.

***REMOVED******REMOVED*** 2. Econophysicist hat — Phase 2 (S&P 500 daily returns)

Phase 2 is the most-contested phase of the paper because of the lognormal sign issue (which the v0.2 + the lognormal-revised companion now state correctly) and because inverse-cubic-law on stock returns is itself a 25-year disputed claim (Gopikrishnan 1998, Plerou 1999 → counter-claims of lognormal-mixture by LeBaron, Malevergne, Pisarenko, and others).

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 2.1 P0 (must-fix-before-submission)

- **P0-E1. Inverse-cubic law on daily index returns is the weakest published form of the claim — the strong form is on individual stocks and on shorter timescales.** Gopikrishnan et al. 1998 fit the inverse cubic on a *mix* of individual-stock and index data, and the later Plerou et al. 1999 and Gabaix et al. 2003 papers establish the law on *individual companies* and on tick-by-tick / minute-resolution data, where n_tail is in the 10⁵–10⁶ range. C1 fits it on n_tail = 2,327 from one index (S&P 500) at one resolution (daily). The α = 2.998 ± 0.041 result is statistically beautiful at this n, but a referee will ask: "Why daily-only, why index-only, why one window?" The current §3.2 does not address this. **Recommended fix: add a sentence in §3.2 explicitly noting that the canonical inverse-cubic law strongest form is on individual stocks at sub-daily resolution, and that the C1 measurement is the daily-index transfer of the law (which has independent published confirmation in Weber et al. 2007, Petersen et al. 2010, and others). This is a defensive sentence, not a methodology change.**
- **P0-E2. Lognormal-favored R = −6.12 is now stated correctly, but the framing in §6.1 should reference the historical lognormal-vs-power-law dispute, not present it as a fresh issue.** The companion document `C1-v0.2-phase2-lognormal-revised-2026-05-24.md` does some of this — it cites Clauset §6.3 for the limited-power regime — but the framing "the SOC verdict rests on the joint signature, not on rejecting the lognormal" reads as a defensive maneuver to a referee who knows the 2005–2015 econophysics debate. A real econophysicist would expect at least one of {LeBaron 2001, Malevergne et al. 2005, Pisarenko & Sornette 2006, Klass et al. 2006} to be cited as prior art on the lognormal alternative for stock returns. **Recommended fix: add 1–2 lognormal-vs-power-law econophysics references to the §6.1 paragraph in addition to Clauset 2009. The Pisarenko & Sornette piece is the most directly relevant.**
- **P0-E3. Omori p = 0.286 on the daily band is a non-trivial value but the source paper's R² = 0.71 is low.** Phase 1 reports R² = 0.9927 over three decades; Phase 2 reports R² = 0.71 on (one expects) fewer decades. A referee will look at this and ask: "Is the Omori claim on stock returns actually a clean Omori, or is it a slope-zero rejection that happens to land in the daily-band range?" The source paper claims "the slope is far from zero — the flat-rate null is rejected at ≈18σ" for DeFi but does not give the equivalent statistic for S&P 500. **Recommended fix: report the slope-zero null rejection significance for the Phase 2 Omori fit, parallel to the DeFi numbers. If it is comparable to Phase 1 (≫ 10σ), the qualitative claim stands; if it is marginal (~ 3–5σ), the language should soften.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 2.2 P1 (should-address)

- **P1-E1. The "exponent-band agreement" criterion is being asked to do a lot of work.** §3.2 and §6.1 now lean on α = 2.998 vs canonical 3.0 as the decisive criterion for Phase 2's SOC verdict. But the canonical 3.0 is itself an *empirical* number from Gopikrishnan et al. — not a theoretically derived exponent — and there is no theory paper that derives α = 3 for a price-impact-cascade SOC universality class from first principles. A statistical-physics referee will notice this. **Recommended fix: state explicitly in §3.2 that the "canonical 3.0" is the empirical inverse-cubic-law value, not a theoretically derived SOC exponent, and that the C1 claim is "the S&P 500 daily return tail reproduces the empirical inverse-cubic value to within one analytic standard error" rather than "the S&P 500 reproduces the SOC-predicted exponent." The two are different in kind.**
- **P1-E2. Single-window concern (§6.5 calls it out generically).** 1990–2025 is one 35-year window. Splitting it into the 1990–2007 (pre-GFC) and 2008–2025 (post-GFC) sub-periods and showing the α is stable across both would be a much stronger result. The source paper does not do this. **Recommended fix: a follow-up sub-period stability check would be a strong v0.3 addition. If not done before submission, §6.5 should be sharper about this specifically (not just "one window").**
- **P1-E3. Why daily |r|, not r² or signed r?** The source paper §2.2 mentions |r| is the established observable but does not say what happens if r² or signed r is used. A referee may ask. **Recommended fix: add a one-sentence robustness note ("identical fits on r² give α = 1 + (3 − 1)/2 = 2.0 ± ε, consistent with the |r| → r² Jacobian transformation").**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 2.3 P2 (nice-to-have)

- **P2-E1. Mention Mantegna–Stanley levy-flight literature.** The competing model to inverse-cubic-law SOC on stock returns is Mantegna–Stanley's truncated Lévy distribution, which is power-law in the body and exponential in the far tail. A footnote acknowledging this competing model would strengthen the paper.
- **P2-E2. yfinance data hygiene.** The source paper uses yfinance to fetch S&P 500 daily closes. There is a known issue that yfinance occasionally has dividend-adjustment errors. **Recommended fix: state that close prices were adjusted (or not) and whether dividend-adjustment was applied. Likely doesn't change α, but auditable.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 2.4 The hardest single question an econophysics referee will ask

> *"Your R = −6.12 against lognormal is statistically robust (p = 9.3e-10) and your α = 2.998 matches Gopikrishnan to four decimals. You are reporting both results and adjudicating between them using a 'joint-signature' criterion. What would falsify the SOC verdict for S&P 500? Is there any combination of tail-exponent and Vuong-test outcomes that would make you say no?"*

This is the question that decides whether the verdict is principled or motivated. The defensible answer is: "If α drifted outside the canonical inverse-cubic band [2.6, 3.4] *and* the lognormal-favoring direction held *and* the Omori p fell outside the published daily band, that joint failure would falsify Phase 2's SOC membership." The current draft does not state any such falsifier explicitly. **Recommended fix: add a sentence in §6.1 stating what joint-signature failure mode would falsify Phase 2's SOC verdict.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED*** 3. Neuroscientist hat — Phase 4 (mouse ALM cortex avalanches)

Phase 4 is the most epistemically defensible of the three because the source paper *already* concedes "the absolute exponents are not Beggs–Plenz" and reframes the claim around the γ scaling relation. But neural-avalanche analysis is a notoriously methodology-sensitive field (Touboul–Destexhe, Priesemann subsampling, Beggs–Timme "being critical of criticality"), and a neuroscientist referee will push hard on specifics.

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 3.1 P0 (must-fix-before-submission)

- **P0-N1. Single session, single animal — this is acknowledged in §6.5 but the framing should be sharper.** A neuroscience referee reading "1.39 M spikes from 71 sorted units" will immediately register: that is *one* session. DANDI 000006 itself contains multiple sessions / animals; the source paper §2.1 reports running only `sub-anm369962_ses-20170313`. The strong test is *cross-session* / *cross-animal* stability of γ. Without that, the claim "the cortex is in a task-active sub-critical sub-class" rests on n = 1. **Recommended fix: either (a) run the same pipeline on 2–3 additional sessions from DANDI 000006 — the source paper's analysis script `analyze_avalanches.py` can be re-run, this is CC-fixable — or (b) sharpen §6.5 to explicitly say "n = 1 session" and treat Phase 4's verdict as preliminary in Table 1. Currently Table 1 says "scaling relation γ ≈ 1.10 holds" without flagging the single-session caveat.**
- **P0-N2. Avalanche detection threshold — empty-bin definition.** §2.2 of the source paper defines an avalanche as "a maximal run of consecutive non-empty bins bordered by at least one empty bin on each side." This is the standard Beggs–Plenz definition but is known to be sensitive to (i) bin width (covered by the bin-factor sweep), and (ii) the *pooling* of spikes across units before binning. The source paper does pool ("we pool all spikes across units into a single sorted time series"). Pooled-spike avalanche detection vs. per-unit avalanche detection give different exponents; Priesemann et al. 2014 specifically warn that pooling can mask subcritical structure. **Recommended fix: state in §3.4 (or in the Phase 4 methods of C1) that the avalanche detection is on *pooled* spikes across all 71 units, and either (a) report that per-unit detection gives consistent γ — preferred, requires re-run — or (b) cite Priesemann et al. as the source of the pooled-spike methodology and acknowledge its limitation.**
- **P0-N3. γ ≈ 1.10 vs the theoretical mean-field prediction γ = 2.** The source paper §3.3 reports the scaling relation as satisfied "to within 2–3%" with measured γ ≈ 1.10 and "(α_T − 1)/(τ − 1)" matching. The mean-field branching-process theoretical prediction is γ = 2 (Friedman et al. 2012). γ = 1.10 is *very far* from γ = 2. A neuroscience referee will read this and immediately ask: "Wait — your scaling relation holds, but at γ = 1.10 not γ = 2. Are you using a different definition of γ?" **This is a real issue.** The "scaling relation" formulation (α_T − 1)/(τ − 1) is consistent regardless of the absolute γ value, but calling γ = 1.10 "confirmation of criticality" without addressing the deviation from γ_MF = 2 is a serious framing problem. **Recommended fix: in §3.4 of C1, state explicitly: "The measured γ ≈ 1.10 differs from the mean-field branching-process prediction γ = 2; the cross-bin stability of γ (and its agreement with (α_T − 1)/(τ − 1)) is the criticality signature, while the absolute value γ ≈ 1.10 places the recording outside the mean-field universality fixed point. This is consistent with the steeper tail exponents τ ∈ [2.17, 3.00] vs the mean-field τ = 3/2, and reinforces the task-active sub-class interpretation." Without this clarification a Beggs–Timme-style referee will reject on inconsistent framing.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 3.2 P1 (should-address)

- **P1-N1. Subsampling correction not applied.** The source paper §5.4 lists this as a limitation ("a formal subsampling correction [Priesemann] would sharpen interpretation"). 71 sorted units is a small subset of the underlying network (~ 10⁵ neurons in mouse ALM). Priesemann et al. 2014 show that subsampling alone can shift τ from 3/2 toward 5/2 — exactly the direction the data drift in. **A neuroscience referee will ask: how do you know your τ ∈ [2.17, 3.00] is a task-active sub-critical signature vs a pure subsampling artifact?** This is the single biggest reframe risk in the paper. **Recommended fix: in §3.4 of C1 and §6.1 / §6.4 of the limitations, state explicitly that the observed τ shift is consistent with both (a) task-active sub-critical state per Priesemann/Shew, and (b) subsampling-only artifact per Priesemann 2014, and that distinguishing the two would require either a higher unit-count recording or a formal subsampling correction. Currently the draft attributes the shift unambiguously to task-active sub-criticality — an over-claim.**
- **P1-N2. Branching ratio m not measured.** The source paper §5.5 acknowledges this. The directly-fittable quantity that would settle critical-vs-subcritical-vs-supercritical is the branching ratio m (Priesemann's MR estimator or any of the multi-step-ahead methods). Without it, the "task-active sub-critical" claim is an inference from τ, not a direct measurement. **Recommended fix: either run the MR-estimator from the `mrestimator` Python package on the same data — CC-fixable — or sharpen the language to "consistent with sub-critical state" rather than "places the recording in a sub-critical sub-class."**
- **P1-N3. Synthetic Bienaymé–Galton–Watson validation recovers τ = 1.497 (vs 1.500 predicted) but α_T = 1.917 (vs 2.000 predicted).** The α_T deviation of 0.083 (vs ±0.005 reported error) is *not* "negligible" — it is 16σ on the analytic error. The source paper Table 1 reports this matter-of-factly. A referee will notice. **Recommended fix: investigate whether the α_T deviation is (a) a finite-N effect at n = 200,000 (probably, mean-field α_T converges slowly), (b) a binning-edge effect, or (c) a real pipeline bias. State which.**

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 3.3 P2 (nice-to-have)

- **P2-N1. ALM is a motor cortex area; the canonical Beggs–Plenz preparations are V1 / S1 / cultured tissue. Different cortical areas can exhibit different SOC sub-classes. A footnote acknowledging the area-specificity would help.
- **P2-N2. Delay-response task structure not exploited.** The recording has trial structure (delay, cue, response). Trial-phase-resolved analysis was flagged in the source paper §5.2 as a follow-up. Mentioning it in C1 §6.5 would strengthen the limitations framing.

***REMOVED******REMOVED******REMOVED*** 3.4 The hardest single question a neuroscience referee will ask

> *"You measure τ ∈ [2.17, 3.00] on a 71-unit subsampling of mouse ALM during a behavioral task. Priesemann et al. 2014 explicitly show that subsampling alone shifts τ from 3/2 to ~3 in the absence of any sub-critical-state shift. How do you exclude subsampling as the explanation for your tail shift, and what would your pipeline measure if you (or DANDI 000006) had a 10× higher unit yield?"*

This is the single most dangerous question for Phase 4. The defensible answer is: "We cannot exclude subsampling-only with the current single-session 71-unit recording; the verdict for Phase 4 is consistency-with-task-active-sub-critical, not an unambiguous identification, and the cross-bin γ stability is the criticality signature that is invariant under subsampling per Friedman et al. 2012." **Recommended fix: add this to §3.4 or §6.1.** The current draft does not have this caveat.
