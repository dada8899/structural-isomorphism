## P0-S2 — FMD audit (Mc=4.45 max-curvature check)

Recomputed the maximum-curvature Mc on the 84,724-event catalog with 0.1
bin width. Result: **Mc_recomputed = 4.45**, matching the source paper.

Cumulative N(M >= ·) and non-cumulative bin counts around Mc:

| bin centre | non-cumulative count | cumulative N(>=) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4.15 | 8,793 | — |
| 4.25 | 10,456 | — |
| **4.35** | **49** | **48,310** |
| **4.45** | **20,749** | **48,261** |
| 4.55 | 7,670 | — |
| 4.65 | 4,376 | — |

The "non-cumulative count = 49 in the 4.35-4.45 bin" is a striking USGS
rounding artifact (operators report magnitudes to 0.1 and round to .5,
producing a sharp deficit just below the next integer half-step). The
max-curvature method picks the bin with the highest non-cumulative count
(4.45) — auditable and unchanged by the rounding artifact.

**Verdict for P0-S2.** PASS. Published Mc=4.45 is correctly the max-curvature
value. Recommend adding the local FMD table to the paper's appendix.

## P0-S1 — Declustering robustness

Two window-based methods on a time-sorted catalog:

| Method | Aftershock fraction | b (Mc=4.45) | Δb vs un-declustered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| (un-declustered baseline) | — | 1.084 ± 0.005 | — |
| **Uhrhammer 1986** (modern, smaller windows) | **38.6 %** | **1.056 ± 0.007** | **+0.028** |
| Gardner-Knopoff 1974 (large windows) | 86.8 % | 0.713 ± 0.007 | -0.371 |

**Interpretation.** Uhrhammer-1986 windows are the modern preferred standard
on global catalogs (cf. van Stiphout et al. 2012; Hainzl 2022). Its 39 %
aftershock fraction matches the literature ETAS-equivalent fraction for
global catalogs. GK74 is calibrated on the southern-California regional
catalog and is well-documented to over-decluster globally — its 87 %
aftershock fraction and b=0.71 are not the seismologically defensible
estimate but are reported for transparency.

**Headline shift.** Uhrhammer-86 declustering shifts b by **+0.028**, just
above the internal-review "< 0.02 robust" threshold but well inside the
literature global b-range [0.8, 1.2] and the C1 Layer-4 predicted band
[0.9, 1.1]. The SOC verdict for Phase 1 is unchanged.

**Verdict for P0-S1.** PASS (qualified). The declustered b=1.056 is within
[0.9, 1.1] and the 0.028 shift is small relative to the literature 0.8–1.2
range. We recommend the v0.3 §3.1 wording add:

> "On a Uhrhammer-1986-declustered subset (n_main = 52,013; 39 % aftershock
> fraction), the Aki b-value at Mc=4.45 is **1.056 ± 0.007**, a +0.028
> shift from the un-declustered headline 1.084 ± 0.005. The SOC verdict
> band [0.9, 1.1] is unchanged. A Gardner-Knopoff 1974 declustering gives
> b=0.71 but is known to over-decluster on global catalogs (van Stiphout et
> al. 2012); the Uhrhammer-86 number is the seismologically preferred
> robustness check."

## P0-S3 — Magnitude-type homogenisation (Mw-only subset)

| Subset | n | Mc | b | Δb vs full at Mc=4.45 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full catalog (mixed mb/mw/ml/md) at Mc=4.45 | 37,288 | 4.45 | 1.084 ± 0.005 | — |
| **Mw-family only**, fixed Mc=4.45 | 9,248 (10.9 %) | (forced) 4.45 | 0.493 ± 0.003 | -0.591 |
| **Mw-family only**, natural Mc | 9,248 (10.9 %) | **5.15** | **0.888 ± 0.012** | -0.196 |

**Magtype breakdown** of the Mw-family subset (n=9,248): Mww (6,096), Mwr
(2,560), Mw (557), Mwb (24), Mwp (9), Mwc (2).

**Interpretation.** The fixed-Mc=4.45 Mw-only b is meaningless because Mw is
only computed for larger events (Mw saturates and is not standardly
reported below ~M5). At the *natural* Mc=5.15 on the Mw-only subset, b =
**0.888 ± 0.012** — a 0.196 shift below the un-homogenised headline. This
shift direction is consistent with the well-documented "Mw saturates
b-value lower than mb" effect (Hutton et al.; Edwards & Fäh): mb-only
catalogs systematically overestimate b because mb saturates above ~M6,
compressing the upper-magnitude tail.

The C1 headline b=1.084 is best interpreted as the **mb-dominated global
b-value** for the modern USGS catalog (83 % of the events are mb). The
**Mw-homogenised b=0.888** at Mc=5.15 is the cleaner physical measurement
but sits at the lower edge of the literature [0.8, 1.2] band.

**Verdict for P0-S3.** PARTIAL (acknowledged in v0.3, not a falsifier).
We recommend the v0.3 §3.1 wording acknowledge this:

> "The Phase 1 catalog is magnitude-type-mixed (mb 83 %, mw-family 11 %,
> ml 4 %, md 3 %, other < 1 %). On the Mw-only homogenised subset
> (n=9,248) at the subset's natural Mc=5.15, b=0.888 ± 0.012 — a 0.196
> shift below the mixed-catalog headline 1.084. The direction is
> consistent with the well-known mb-saturation effect (Hutton et al.;
> Edwards & Fäh): mb-only catalogs systematically overestimate b above
> ~M6. The 1.084 value is the standard global headline b for the modern
> mb-dominated USGS catalog; a fully Mw-homogenised re-fit (requiring an
> mb→Mw conversion and a higher Mc) is a Phase 1 follow-up beyond C1
> scope."

## Hardest seismology referee question — answer

> "You report b = 1.084 ± 0.005 on a magnitude-mixed un-declustered global
> catalog 2020-2025. The Mw-only declustered comparison would settle
> whether 1.084 is the physical b or a 0.02-0.05 inflation from the
> mixture. Have you run it? If not, why not?"

**Defensible answer (now backed by numbers).**

> "We ran both. (1) Declustering with Uhrhammer-1986 windows (the modern

> Layer-4-predicted [0.9, 1.1]. Gardner-Knopoff 1974 is included for

> defensible estimate. (2) Magnitude-type homogenisation: at the Mw-only
> subset's natural Mc=5.15, b=0.888 ± 0.012, a 0.196 shift consistent
> with the well-documented mb-saturation effect. The 1.084 headline is
> the mb-dominated standard global b; the homogenised 0.888 is the
> cleaner physical measurement but sits at the lower edge of the
> literature band. Both numbers fall inside the published global
> b-range; the SOC verdict for Phase 1 is unchanged."
