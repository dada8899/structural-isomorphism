**Cross-session statistics (n=5):**

| Quantity | Mean | Std (SE_x5) | Range | n |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| τ (size exponent) | **2.949** | **0.037** | [2.906, 2.988] | 5 |
| α_T (duration exponent) | **2.811** | **0.108** | [2.705, 2.973] | 5 |
| γ measured (s ∝ T^γ) | **1.107** | **0.011** | [1.098, 1.125] | 5 |
| γ predicted from (α_T-1)/(τ-1) | 0.929 | 0.047 | [0.886, 0.992] | 5 |

**Key findings.**

1. **τ is extraordinarily stable cross-session.** Mean 2.949, std 0.037 —
   that is a 1.3 % cross-session spread. Cross-animal stability holds:
   different animals (anm369962/3/4) give τ values within the same band
   2.91-2.99.
2. **α_T varies more (std 0.11, 3.9 %)** but stays inside [2.7, 3.0].
3. **γ measured is exceptionally stable: 1.107 ± 0.011**, the most
   reproducible quantity across the 5 sessions. This is the "criticality
   signature" the source paper relies on, and it survives multi-session
   replication cleanly.
4. **γ measured (1.107) ≠ γ predicted from (α_T-1)/(τ-1) (0.929).**
   Difference 0.18, consistent across sessions (the gap is not noise; it
   is a systematic). This already was a P0-N3 framing issue in the
   internal review (γ_measured vs γ_MF = 2.0).

**Interpretation for the multi-session question.** The single-session
verdict generalises. The Phase 4 result is *not* an artifact of one
animal or one recording — τ ≈ 2.95 ± 0.04 and γ ≈ 1.11 ± 0.01 are
animal-invariant within DANDI:000006. The "task-active sub-critical
sub-class" claim is at least 5-session-stable, with cross-session SE
on τ of 0.04 — well below the 0.3 width of the Beggs-Plenz literature
band [1.5, 3.0] (the band the C1 verdict uses, not the strict mean-field
τ=1.5).

**Verdict for P0-N1.** PASS (qualified). The single-session caveat in v0.2
§6.5 is now over-cautious. We recommend Table 1 + §6.5 wording change to:

> "Cross-session/animal stability checked on n=5 sessions, 3 animals from
> DANDI:000006. Cross-session τ = 2.95 ± 0.04, γ_measured = 1.11 ± 0.01.
> The γ value is the most stable quantity; τ stability holds within 1.3 %
> across animals. Subsampling caveat (P0-N2 below) still applies."

## P0-N2 — Pooled vs per-unit avalanche detection

Same bin width (pooled <IEI>) applied to per-unit spike trains; each unit's
avalanches detected independently and concatenated.

| Session | Pooled n_aval | Per-unit n_aval | Pooled τ | Per-unit τ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20170313 | 301,092 | 1,368,098 | 2.984 | **3.000 (xmin=1, saturated)** |
| 20170314 | 173,613 | 859,497 | 2.946 | **3.000 (saturated)** |
| 20170317 | 201,510 | 940,026 | 2.988 | **3.000 (saturated)** |
| 20170227 | 160,257 | 754,562 | 2.921 | **3.000 (saturated)** |
| 20170320 | 196,227 | 883,148 | 2.906 | **3.000 (saturated)** |

**Methodological finding.** Per-unit detection at the pooled-IEI bin width
is **degenerate**: each unit's spikes are sparse on the pooled-IEI grid
(< IEI_per_unit ≈ N_units × <IEI_pooled>), so per-unit avalanches are
overwhelmingly singletons. Clauset's powerlaw.Fit converges to xmin=1
and α saturates at the upper bound 3.0 — this is a powerlaw-fit boundary
artifact, not a real physical exponent.

**The correct per-unit detection** would use *each unit's own* <IEI> as
its bin width, which is computationally more involved (n_units × per-unit
binning + careful aggregation). This is beyond a ≤2h rerun budget. We
flag this as a **methodological limitation**, not a counterexample.

**Verdict for P0-N2.** PARTIAL / DEFERRED. The pooled-detection finding is
not contradicted by per-unit detection at fixed bin width, but a
per-unit-IEI-binned analysis is a v0.4 follow-up. The C1 claim should
explicitly say "avalanche detection on **pooled spikes**, per Beggs-Plenz
2003," and cite Priesemann et al. 2014 for the pooling caveat as a
limitation.

Recommended v0.3 wording (§3.4 or methods):

> "Avalanche detection is performed on pooled spikes across all sorted
> units (the standard Beggs-Plenz 2003 method) with bin width = pooled
> mean inter-event interval. A first-pass per-unit detection at the
> same bin width is degenerate (avalanches collapse to singletons); a
> proper per-unit-IEI-binned re-analysis is left to follow-up work. The
> cross-session stability of τ and γ holds within the pooled-detection
> methodology; pooling-vs-per-unit interpretation differences (Priesemann
> et al. 2014) remain an open caveat alongside the unmeasured branching
> ratio."

## Hardest neuroscience referee question — partial answer

> "You measure τ ∈ [2.17, 3.00] on a 71-unit subsampling of mouse ALM
> during a behavioral task. Priesemann et al. 2014 explicitly show that
> subsampling alone shifts τ from 3/2 to ~3 in the absence of any
> sub-critical-state shift. How do you exclude subsampling as the
> explanation for your tail shift, and what would your pipeline measure
> if you (or DANDI 000006) had a 10× higher unit yield?"

**Defensible answer with multi-session evidence (now stronger than v0.2):**

> "We cannot exclude subsampling-only with the available unit count.
> However: (a) we now have 5-session 3-animal cross-validation showing
> τ = 2.95 ± 0.04 — a < 4 % cross-session spread inconsistent with
> subsampling-only being the *only* driver, since per-session unit yields
> vary from 42 to 71 and τ stays within 0.08. If subsampling were the
> dominant determinant of τ, lower-unit sessions should show systematically
> larger τ; we don't see that. (b) γ_measured = 1.107 ± 0.011, the
> cross-bin scaling-relation signature that is subsampling-invariant per
> Friedman et al. 2012, is the criticality test we lean on. (c) A
> formal subsampling correction (Priesemann's MR-estimator) and a
> higher-density recording are flagged as v0.4 work."
