***REMOVED*** C1 vs thirteen-system sibling — submission-time decision memo

**Question.** When C1 is submitted to arXiv, should the thirteen-system sibling manuscript (`unified-pipeline-v0.2-2026-05-13`) be co-submitted as a *separate* arXiv preprint at the same time, or should C1 ship alone and the sibling be held back to a later (or never)?

The scope decision in §1 of C1 v0.2 fixes C1 as the *focused* five-system SOC paper. This memo does not re-open that decision. It answers the orthogonal question of whether the broader sibling paper should be posted *alongside* C1.

---

***REMOVED******REMOVED*** Pro (post both, simultaneously)

1. **The two papers have genuinely different theses.** C1 says: "one SOC universality class verified deeply across four real domains plus a null." The sibling says: "one *methodological framework* shown to be portable across five class families (SOC threshold-cascade, preferential attachment, Motter–Lai network cascade, Preisach hysteresis, Scheffer fold-bifurcation)." Neither subsumes the other.
2. **Reduces fragmentation accusation.** A reviewer who sees C1 alone might fairly ask: "Why did the authors silo the SOC story when their pipeline runs on thirteen systems?" Co-posting answers that pre-emptively. The sibling paper's existence is already referenced in C1 §1 ("the thirteen-system sibling manuscript"); not posting it leaves a dangling forward reference.
3. **Cross-citation strength.** Posted together, each paper can cite the other by arXiv ID; the lognormal sign correction in C1 §3.2 (where C1 explicitly relies on the sibling reading R = −6.12 correctly) becomes a citable cross-reference rather than an internal claim.
4. **Roadmap alignment.** W7-A roadmap §5–6 describes the publication strategy as "two complementary preprints — one focused, one wide" rather than as a sequence. Sequential posting introduces an unnecessary delay window during which only one of the two halves of the strategy is on the record.
5. **Single submission window is cheap.** arXiv submission and (later) journal submission are largely fixed-cost on author time; doing them in parallel is not 2× the effort.

***REMOVED******REMOVED*** Con (post C1 only, hold or drop the sibling)

1. **The thirteen-system sibling is broader and has more failure modes.** It adds Phases 6–13 plus the two non-SOC classes (Preisach hysteresis on NGSIM traffic, Scheffer fold-bifurcation on Fox River DO). Several of those phases use literature-meta catalogs or single-window analyses (cf. C1 §6.5) and have not been through the same independent re-fit cycle as Phases 1–5. A referee can attack one weak Phase to weaken the whole paper, and the sibling currently has 8+ Phases vs C1's 4 real systems plus null.
2. **Lognormal-direction issue is not unique to Phase 2.** The sibling adds the v0.2 log-binned BIC test that supports the SOC verdict where the raw-tail Vuong test does not. C1 already cites that. But the sibling's universal-collapse claim (§4) and its BIC test are themselves newer machinery that has not received independent peer scrutiny. Posting it simultaneously with C1 exposes C1 to collateral risk if the sibling's BIC test is attacked.
3. **Reviewer-burden asymmetry.** A focused four-real-systems-plus-null paper is the easier referee assignment; the sibling needs a referee comfortable across five class families, which is hard to find. Holding the sibling lets it be revised with C1's referee feedback before going out.
4. **Provenance caveat is sharper in sibling.** C1 §2 already flags the "339 lines / commit 7ee228c" description in the sibling as stale relative to the package-based layout. Co-posting requires either updating the sibling's pipeline section to match C1's package-based description or accepting that two simultaneously-posted preprints describe their shared pipeline inconsistently.
5. **arXiv etiquette.** Two preprints with overlapping numerical claims (the sibling reproduces C1's Phase 1–4 numbers verbatim) posted in the same week from the same author can read as either "comprehensive" or "self-plagiarising," depending on referee mood. There is no formal arXiv rule against it, but it is a soft signal worth weighing.

---

***REMOVED******REMOVED*** Recommendation (Claude)

**Post C1 first; hold the sibling for ~6–8 weeks; then post the sibling as a separate preprint that cites the C1 arXiv ID.**

Rationale:

- **C1's value-add is the focused thesis.** "One pipeline, four real domains, one null" is the most defensible single claim the project can make in 2026-05. Submitting it first establishes the methodological core on the record.
- **The sibling depends on C1 epistemically.** The sibling's lognormal-correction discussion, its Phase 1–5 numbers, and its v0.2 BIC machinery all *depend on* C1's groundwork being on the record. The forward-citation flow is sibling → C1, not the reverse. That is a natural reason for the sibling to follow, not lead.
- **Provenance reconciliation is cleaner in sequence.** C1 sets the canonical `soc-pipeline-v0.1.0` tag and the package-based pipeline description (§2 + Appendix A). The sibling's stale "339 lines / commit 7ee228c" wording is then a localized cleanup before its own submission, not a blocker for C1.
- **The 6–8-week window is the natural revision window after arXiv posting** (response to public-comment / community feedback). Holding the sibling slightly behind lets it absorb whatever C1's posting surfaces — known reviewer concerns about cross-domain SOC papers (small-n, lognormal alternative, single-session neural) will mostly attach to *both* papers, and revising the sibling once after C1 is posted is cheaper than revising both in lockstep.
- **One unambiguous case where this recommendation flips:** if W7-A milestone work (universal-collapse 13-system + log-binned BIC) is *fully baked and independently reviewed* at C1 submission time, simultaneous posting becomes safer and the pro side of the analysis dominates. As of 2026-05-24 the sibling has not been through that gate.
