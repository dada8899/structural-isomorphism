Cross-domain "universality" claims -- that earthquakes, financial markets, neural avalanches, wildfires and solar flares share a single critical mechanism -- are perennially over-generated by surface-similarity heuristics, and the failure mode is amplified when the curator is an LLM. We describe and evaluate a reject-aware pipeline that treats false-positive control as a first-class design goal alongside positive discovery. The pipeline is organised as three sequential layers (extract / curate / critic+predict) and inserts two adversarial critic stages -- B1, a single Opus reviewer, and B3, a three-decoding DeepSeek ensemble -- between LLM curation and quantitative-prediction generation. The substantive methodological contribution is a reject-aware filter that surfaces a particular class of false positive: mathematical frameworks masquerading as universality classes (delay-differential normal forms, tail copulas, scale-free percolation as a single class, Preisach hysteresis monolith). On a 21-candidate panel, B1 alone rejected 3/21 (14.3%); B3 rejected 7/21 (33.3%), more than doubling the false-positive flagging rate. Four of the seven B3 rejections were classes B1 had voted KEEP -- the most consequential disagreements concentrate where B1 was overconfident, including a textbook mechanism-vs-limit-theorem confusion. Two of seven B3 rejections were independently corroborated by downstream real-data tests. We frame B3 honestly as within-vendor multi-decoding rather than cross-architecture ensemble; the lift over B1 confounds vendor switch and decoding diversity, and a planned cross-family extension (B4) would disambiguate these. Costs are under $1 per 21-class panel -- three orders of magnitude under expert-human reviewer time. The code, prompts, and verdict matrix are publicly available; the methodology is reproducible end-to-end at sub-dollar marginal cost per panel.
