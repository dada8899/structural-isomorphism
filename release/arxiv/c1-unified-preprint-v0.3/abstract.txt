A universality-class claim has empirical content only if a single fixed
pipeline -- with no per-domain tuning -- recovers the predicted scaling
across systems from different domains AND fails to find it in matched
non-class data. We assemble such a pipeline (Clauset-Shalizi-Newman
2009 MLE power-law with KS-driven x_min, likelihood-ratio tests against
lognormal and exponential, Omori-Utsu decay stacking) and apply it
unchanged to five systems: USGS earthquakes (Phase 1), S&P 500 daily
returns (Phase 2), DeFi liquidation cascades across three protocols
(Phase 3), mouse-cortex neural avalanches (Phase 4), and four synthetic
non-SOC nulls (Phase 5). The pipeline recovers canonical exponents: a
Gutenberg-Richter b = 1.084 +/- 0.005 on 37,281 earthquakes (Omori p =
0.941); an inverse-cubic tail alpha = 2.998 +/- 0.041 on 9,060 S&P 500
returns; alpha in [1.57, 1.68] across 43,065 DeFi liquidations (Aave V2,
Compound V2, MakerDAO; Omori p in [0.69, 0.76]); and an SOC scaling
relation satisfied to within 2% on 1.39M mouse-cortex spikes (gamma ~
1.10), although the recording's tau in [2.17, 3.00] places it in a
task-active sub-class. All four nulls are rejected (LR -16 to -45, Omori
R^2 ~ 0.002), ruling out the failure mode "fits everything as a power
law". Three honest caveats: (i) the lognormal is not rejected in every
raw-tail test (S&P 500 R = -6.12); (ii) the pipeline detects endogenous
threshold-cascade signatures only; (iii) downstream cross-domain
predictions remain unverified. Within those limits, the result is a
single-pipeline cross-domain test of SOC universality across geophysics,
equity finance, DeFi, and neuroscience.
